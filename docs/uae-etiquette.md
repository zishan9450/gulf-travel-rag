# Etiquette in the UAE

The UAE is used to visitors and still expects guest behaviour in mosques, malls, and government buildings. Cover shoulders and knees for the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and for a majlis. In a mall, beachwear is for the beach; a cover-up is the normal walk between the car and the shop. Ramadan rules match the rest of the Gulf: no eating or drinking in public during the fast, including in a car with the windows down at a light.

## Photographs and the public

Ask before you photograph a person. Do not photograph accidents, police, or military sites. A drone needs a permit and is a bad thing to unpack on a corniche to "see if it works".

## Alcohol, emirate by emirate

Hotel bars in Dubai and Abu Dhabi serve alcohol to guests. Sharjah is a dry emirate: do not carry alcohol into it, and do not expect a hotel there to serve it. Drinking in a public park, a beach, or a taxi is an offence even in Dubai. What is allowed inside a licensed venue is not allowed on the pavement outside it.

## Friday and the weekend

The public weekend in the UAE is Saturday and Sunday. Friday is a working day for many offices and the congregational prayer day, so a mosque is busy at midday and a government counter may pause. Dress a little more formally for a government office than for a mall: closed shoes, covered knees.

## A small thing that matters

The right hand is for handing over a gift, a card, or a cup. Pointing the sole of your shoe at someone when you sit is poor form. None of this is complicated. It is the same care you would take in a host's living room.

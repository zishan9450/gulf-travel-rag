# Salalah and Khareef

Salalah, in the Dhofar governorate, is the place Oman sends you when the rest of the country is too hot. Khareef runs from June to September. Moist air off the Arabian Sea hits the mountains, the hills turn green, and waterfalls appear on roads that are dry the rest of the year. The season is crowded. Hotels in central Salalah book out, and the coastal road fills with visitors from across the Gulf who come for the mist.

## What to do while it is green

Drive the mountain road toward the springs and the viewpoints, and expect fog thick enough that you will want headlights at noon. Walking paths get muddy. A light rain jacket is more useful than a desert sun hat during these weeks. The coconut plantations and the banana groves below the escarpment stay green longer than the high ridges.

## The rest of the year

Outside Khareef, Salalah is warm, sunny, and much quieter. The beaches east and west of town are long and open. Winter is an easy time for the coast. Summer outside the green zone, meaning the inland desert on the way down from Muscat, is still extremely hot, so do not judge the whole drive by the weather you meet in the city.

## Practical notes

Salalah has its own airport, which saves the long desert road from Muscat if you only have a few days. The frankincense trade is the old story of this coast; the museum and the port are the sober version, and the souq is the one you can smell. Friday remains a slow morning for shops.

## What to skip in July

A plan that says "empty beaches and no crowds" does not match Khareef. If you want Salalah to yourself, come outside June to September and accept brown hills instead of a green monsoon.

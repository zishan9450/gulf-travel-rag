# Sharqiya Sands

The orange dunes east of the Muscat–Sur road, once known as Wahiba Sands, are now called Sharqiya Sands. The new name is the one you will see on road signs. Desert camps sit on the edge of the dunes and in the middle of them. A standard camp has a sleeping tent, a shared bathroom block, and dinner on a carpet. A camp that advertises a private pool is a different price and a different road in.

## How to arrive

A normal car can reach the camps that sit on the graded entrance tracks. The moment the track becomes soft sand, you want a 4x4 and a driver who has deflated the tyres. Self-driving into the high dunes without that experience is how visitors get a long tow. Many camps will meet you at a paved rendezvous if you do not want to drive the last part.

## What the night is like

The reason to stay is the evening and the early morning: the heat drops, the stars are obvious, and the dune crests are walkable. Midday in summer is for staying under the shade net. A morning camel ride or a short dune walk is enough for most people. Quad bikes are noisy and easy to overturn on a slip face; take the guide's route, not a race line.

## When to go

November to March is the comfortable season. Summer nights can still be warm, and a June crossing of the open sand at noon is a bad plan. Khareef does not turn these dunes green. That weather belongs to Dhofar, hundreds of kilometres south. Pack a layer for the wind after dark even in winter, and keep a scarf for blowing sand on the crest.

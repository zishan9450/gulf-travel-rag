# What to pack

Pack for air conditioning and for sun, not for one temperature. Malls, mosques, and hotel lobbies are cold. The street is not. A light scarf is worth packing for both Oman and the UAE: it covers hair at a mosque, keeps sun off the neck, and blocks dust on a dune. Women and men both use one.

## Clothing

Bring one outfit that covers shoulders and knees, in a fabric that dries, and wear it on the day you visit a mosque or a fort. Swimwear is for the pool and the beach. A long shirt over swimwear solves the walk to a beach cafe. Closed shoes handle the souq and the fort stairs; sandals handle everything else. One smarter shirt is enough for the Royal Opera House or a palace visit.

## Small things that solve the day

Sunscreen, a hat, and a reusable bottle matter from April onward. A cheap plug adaptor covers the UK-style sockets used across the region. Cash in small notes helps the abra, the market, and a tip. Cards cover hotels and malls. A photocopy or a phone photo of your passport saves a problem if the original is in a safe when a desk asks for it.

## What you can leave

A heavy coat, hiking boots for a city week, and a drone you have not registered are dead weight. Alcohol from the airport is a nuisance the moment your plan includes Sharjah. Buy water after you land rather than packing it. If you are only going to Salalah in the monsoon, add a light rain jacket and leave the desert boots at home.

# Abu Dhabi

Abu Dhabi is the capital of the UAE, on its own island, calmer and more spaced out than Dubai. The building to plan around is the white mosque on the approach from the mainland. Entry to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is free. Visitors need a ticket reserved for a time slot, modest clothing, and patience at the security check. Women cover their hair. Abayas are available to borrow, and bringing your own scarf still makes the entrance faster. The mosque is closed to visitors during prayer times.

## The Louvre and the corniche

Louvre Abu Dhabi sits on Saadiyat Island, under the dome that rains light into the galleries. It is a paid museum, unlike the mosque, and it is worth a timed morning rather than a rushed hour. The Corniche is the seafront promenade of the main island: a cycle path, beaches with separate swimming hours, and a skyline that is best at the end of the day. Swimming areas are marked. A hotel beach and a public beach are not the same entrance.

## Qasr Al Watan and the date of your visit

The presidential palace, Qasr Al Watan, is a separate paid visit with its own dress rules and bag checks. It is not inside the mosque complex, even though both are white and formal. Friday is a popular day for the mosque. Arriving exactly at opening, with the reservation on your phone, is the calm version.

## Getting there from Dubai

The drive from Dubai is a bit over an hour outside rush hour and longer at the end of a workday. Buses and private transfers run the route. A plan that stacks the mosque, the Louvre, and a desert camp in one afternoon is too much. Give the mosque its quiet time and put the museum on its own half-day.

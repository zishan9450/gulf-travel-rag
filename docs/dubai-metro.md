# Dubai Metro

The Dubai Metro is the simplest way across the city if your stops are near a station. You buy a Nol card before you ride. A paper ticket is not the normal product. The red card is the tourist one: you pay a small amount for the card and load the trips you want. Tap in and tap out. The fare depends on how many zones you cross, so a card with too little credit will not open the exit gate.

## Lines and hours

The Red Line runs roughly from the airport end of the city toward the marina and Expo. The Green Line loops through the older districts of Deira and Bur Dubai. They meet at Union and BurJuman. The metro does not run 24 hours. Service starts in the early morning and ends around midnight, and on Friday the start is later. Check the day's first train if you have a dawn flight and you were counting on the metro to the airport; a taxi is the backup that actually runs.

## What it does not reach

The Palm, many hotel driveways, and the desert edge are off the lines. A station called a neighbourhood's name can still be a long walk or a feeder bus from the hotel door. In summer that walk is the part of the journey people underestimate. Use the indoor connection through a mall station when there is one.

## Rules that are enforced

Food and drink are not for the train. Priority seating is for the people it names. The Gold class car at the front costs more and is the quieter one. Rush hour on the Red Line toward the marina is crowded; a small suitcase is fine, a stack of boxes is not. Keep the Nol card. You can hand it to a friend or keep it for the next trip, and the machines show the balance.

# Nizwa

Nizwa was the seat of the imamate and it is still the inland town people use as a base for the mountains. The fort's round tower is the landmark, with a well and rooms that show how the place was defended. Give it an hour. The souq wrapped around the fort entrance is the other half of the visit: dates, silver, and pottery from Bahla.

## The Friday market

The Friday livestock market starts after dawn, on the edge of the souq, and it is largely over by mid-morning. Goats are the main trade. You are welcome to watch. Keep out of the handlers' path, and do not expect a craft market at that hour; the animal sale and the silver shops are different events that happen to share a town. Arrive early if the market itself is what you came for. Arriving at noon means you have missed it.

## Around the town

Bahla Fort is a short drive west and is a separate ticket and a separate hour. Jabrin Castle is further and rewards the extra distance with painted ceilings. Both are easier with a car than with the bus, because the intercity coach drops you in Nizwa, not at the castle gate.

## Staying and eating

Nizwa is a practical overnight stop between Muscat and the mountains, not a resort. Restaurants serve grilled meat and rice, and vegetarian travellers do better at lunch than at a late roadside grill. Friday daytime is quiet once the market ends. The fort may keep different hours on Friday, so check the door rather than assuming the weekday schedule.

## Heat

In summer the stone courtyards are harsh by ten in the morning. The worthwhile plan is market at dawn, fort when it opens, and then a drive toward a wadi or back to the coast before the middle of the day.

# Getting around Oman

Distances in Oman are long and the roads are good. Driving is on the right. A foreign driving licence is often accepted for a short tourist stay when it is in English or accompanied by a translation, but the rental desk is the place that will say yes or no for your particular licence. An international driving permit is the document that removes the argument.

## Buses

Mwasalat buses link Muscat with Nizwa, and they also run to Sohar, Sur, and Salalah. The Salalah coach is an overnight ride across the desert, which is cheaper than flying and much slower. Buy the long-distance seat ahead of a holiday weekend. City buses in Muscat are useful along the main corridors and less useful for a late souq hop; a taxi is the realistic evening plan.

## Taxis and the airport

At Muscat airport, use the official taxi desk or a marked car. Unmarked cars outside the terminal are where fare arguments start. Inside town, ask for the meter or agree the price before the wheels move. The airport is west of the corniche, so a "ten minute" claim at rush hour is optimistic.

## Driving habits that matter

Speed cameras are common and the fines are real. Fuel stations are frequent on the Muscat–Nizwa road and much further apart toward the Empty Quarter. Carry water in the car in summer even if the air conditioning works, because a roadside wait is hot. Do not drive a wadi in a storm. A road that was dry an hour ago can become a flood.

## Borders

A rental car hired in Oman is usually not allowed into the UAE, and the reverse is also commonly true. Crossing by land as a passenger is a different question from driving a hired car across. Check the rental agreement before you point the car at the Hatta or Buraimi road.

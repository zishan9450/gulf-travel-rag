# Transport in the UAE

Dubai's metro is covered on its own page. The rest of the country is a driving country. Roads are fast, cameras are everywhere, and the speed on the sign is the speed that is enforced, not a suggestion. A rental car is the straightforward way to see the east coast or to drive to Abu Dhabi on your own clock.

## Taxis

In Abu Dhabi, taxis use meters. The official cars are painted in the city colours and the meter starts when the trip starts. A driver who refuses the meter is a reason to take the next car, not a negotiation. Dubai taxis are also metered. A hotel car that is not a taxi may be a fixed-price booking; ask which one you are stepping into. The airport ranks are the orderly version. A ride-hailing app is the other orderly version, and it shows the price before you commit.

## Between the emirates

Buses connect Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah for much less than a taxi, and they take longer once traffic on the Sharjah road is included. There is no passenger railway between the emirates in this note. A Friday evening return from Abu Dhabi into Dubai, or a weekday evening into Sharjah, is the traffic to avoid.

## Tolls and parking

Dubai's Salik toll gates charge a rental car, and the rental company adds the fee later. Do not be surprised by it. Mall parking is often validated by a purchase. A public beach may use a paid parking app. Take a photo of where you left the car; the garages are large.

## Desert roads

A highway to Al Ain or to the east coast is an easy drive. A sandy track to a wadi pool is not, even when a blog calls it a sedan road. After rain, those tracks flood. Turn around.

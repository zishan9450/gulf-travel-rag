# Sharjah

Sharjah sits on the same coast as Dubai and feels like a different city. It is the emirate of museums. The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization, beside the corniche, is the collection to start with: science, coins, and calligraphy under a large dome. The Arts Museum and the heritage area around Al Hisn fort are a short walk in the cooler hours. Give the museum district a half day rather than a photo stop between Dubai meetings.

## The practical difference

Sharjah does not run a drinking culture. Plan dinner without wine and keep any alcohol you bought in Dubai out of the car when you cross the line. Dress in the museums is the same modest standard as a mosque: shoulders and knees covered. Friday morning is quiet, and Friday midday around the mosques is not the moment to wander a prayer courtyard.

## Moving between Sharjah and Dubai

The road is short on a map and long in the evening. A taxi across the line is metered and can still cost more than you expect once you are sitting in traffic. The buses are the cheaper option if you are not in a hurry. Sharjah's own airport is a point-to-point holiday airport; it is not a second terminal of Dubai International, and the transfer between them is a real journey, not a corridor.

## What to pair it with

A good day is one museum, the corniche, and a slow meal. It is a poor day if you also promised yourself the Burj Khalifa at sunset. The cities are neighbours, and they are still two plans. Come back to Sharjah for the collections, not as a footnote on a Dubai evening.

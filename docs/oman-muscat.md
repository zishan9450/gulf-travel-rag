# Muscat

Muscat is the capital of Oman, stretched along the Gulf of Oman between rocky headlands. The visit that most travellers plan first is the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, which welcomes non-Muslim guests in the morning outside of prayer times. Shoulders, knees, and hair need to be covered. The carpet and the lamp in the main hall are the parts people cross the city to see. Go early; the doors close again before the midday prayer.

## Mutrah

Mutrah Corniche is the curve of waterfront under the watchtower of the old fort. The souq behind it is a working market: silver khanjars, frankincense, and piles of dates. Prices are for bargaining, but the first number is not an insult, so start gently. The corniche is walkable in the evening, when the heat drops and families are out. Midday in summer is a bad time to stroll it.

## Opera and the bay

The Royal Opera House Muscat sits inland from the corniche and publishes its own season. Dress is smart. Tickets are for a specific night, so a "we will decide at the door" plan often fails on a visiting company. Qurum and Shatti Al Qurum are the beach hotels; the water is calm and the public beach access depends on the stretch, so use a signed entrance rather than crossing a hotel terrace.

## Moving around

Taxis and ride apps cover the spread-out neighbourhoods. Agreeing a fare before you sit down still matters in an unmarked car. Driving is on the right, and the highways between Mutrah, the mosque, and the airport are fast. Friday morning is the quietest time on the roads and the slowest time for a government errand.

## A short first day

Morning at the mosque, coffee, then the corniche and the souq once the light is lower. Leave the opera or a long dinner for the evening. Muscat is not a city you finish in a single walk; the sights sit in separate bays.

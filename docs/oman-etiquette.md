# Etiquette in Oman

Oman is easy to travel in if you dress and speak the way you would as a guest in a private house. At the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, visitors are expected to dress modestly, covering shoulders and knees, and women cover their hair with a scarf. The mosque lends coverings when it has them, but arriving already dressed is better than counting on a loan at the door. Shoes come off before the carpet.

## During Ramadan

Eating, drinking, and chewing gum in public during the fasting hours is rude, even if you are not fasting. Hotels serve food in screened rooms. The evening, after people break the fast, is lively, and it is a good time to be invited for coffee. Do not photograph someone who is praying, and do not walk through a prayer line.

## Greetings and photos

A handshake is fine when the other person offers it. If they do not, a hand over the heart and a greeting is enough. Ask before you photograph a person, especially a woman. Forts, souqs, and landscapes are the usual subjects. A military site, a checkpoint, or a palace gate is not.

## Homes and majlis

If you are invited home, take the shoes off where your host does. The right hand is the one for food and for passing a cup. Coffee may be refilled until you tilt the cup to show you have had enough. It is polite to accept at least one cup.

## Alcohol and dress in town

Alcohol is not part of street life. Some hotels serve it to guests; a public beach is not the place for it. In Mutrah and in any fort, keep the same covered shoulders and knees you would wear to the mosque. Swimwear stays at the pool.

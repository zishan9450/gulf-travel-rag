# When to go

The Gulf has two seasons that matter more than the brochure photographs. June to September is extremely hot on the coast of both Oman and the UAE. Daytime temperatures sit well into the forties, the humidity makes the shade feel hotter, and the sensible plan is indoors from late morning until the evening. Sightseeing then means museums, malls, and mosque visits at opening time.

## The good months

November to March is the comfortable window for walking. Days are warm, evenings in the desert are cool, and this is when the camps, the forts, and the corniche walks work. December and January are also when hotel prices and flights from Europe and India peak. Book the Khareef period in Salalah separately: that green season is June to September, the same months the rest of the region is harsh, and it is a local exception caused by the monsoon on the Dhofar mountains.

## Spring and autumn

April, May, and October are the shoulder. The sea is swimmable and the crowds thin, and by late May the midday heat is already serious. A hiking plan in the Musandam mountains or in Jebel Akhdar belongs in the cooler months. A wadi in July is a swimming hole you reach by car, not a canyon you hike at noon.

## A simple rule

If the activity is outdoors and away from air conditioning, prefer November to March everywhere except Salalah in the monsoon. If you are in Dubai in August, put the desert camp at night or skip it. Heat illness is the common mistake, not a rare one.
